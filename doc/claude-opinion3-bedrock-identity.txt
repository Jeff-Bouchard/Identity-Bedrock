### **Critical Problems That Will Kill This Paper**

#### **1. Your "Minimal" Claim Has a Logical Hole**

You claim:

> "Within the standard PPT model, any public authentication system must assume:
>
> 1. One-way functions (⇔ EUF-CMA signatures) for the challenge–response predicate, and
> 2. Agreement on identifier→public-key bindings."

**Problem:** Assumption (2) is *not* a cryptographic primitive—it's a **distributed systems assumption** about consensus.

You've proven:

- ✅ OWF is the minimal *cryptographic* hardness for signatures (Rompel)
- ✅ Signatures are necessary for challenge-response authentication

But you have **not** proven that:

- ❌ Emercoin's PoS + BTC AuxPoW is the *minimal* consensus mechanism for (2)
- ❌ A blockchain-based directory is necessary at all (vs. gossip protocols, DHTs, or even centralized-but-transparent logs like Certificate Transparency)

**Your claim of "theoretical minimum" is therefore incomplete.** You've minimized the crypto layer but haven't proven minimality of the *consensus layer*.

**Fix:** Either:

- **Narrow your claim:** "We instantiate the minimal *cryptographic* primitives (OWF-equivalent signatures) on a specific consensus mechanism (Emercoin) that is *sufficiently* decentralized for our threat model."
- **Or prove minimality of consensus:** Show that *any* system solving the Byzantine agreement problem for public-key bindings requires at least X economic cost or Y network assumptions—and that Emercoin meets this bound.

The first is honest. The second is a PhD thesis.

#### **2. The Emercoin Consensus Model Is Under-Specified**

You write:

> "Emerʼs PoS + Bitcoin-AuxPoW consensus maintains an append-only NVS view."

**Questions a reviewer will ask:**

1. **What is Emercoin's exact PoS algorithm?** Stake-weighted lottery? BFT-style voting? Delegated PoS?
2. **What percentage of EMC is actually staked?** If it's 5%, then controlling PoS is cheap. If it's 80%, that's different.
3. **How much Bitcoin hashrate is actually pointed at Emercoin's AuxPoW?** Is it 0.01% of BTC's total hashrate? 1%? This determines reorg cost.
4. **What is the historical track record?** You mention "March 2023 stress test" and "October 2024 public reorg attempt"—what *actually* happened? Did the attacks fail because the economic cost was too high, or because the attackers gave up for other reasons?

**You need hard numbers:**

- Current EMC staked: X million EMC (Y% of supply)
- Market cap of EMC: $Z
- Cost to acquire 51% of staked EMC: $W
- Bitcoin hashrate devoted to Emercoin AuxPoW: X TH/s (Y% of BTC total)
- Estimated cost to produce 2 fake AuxPoW walls: $Z in BTC mining

Without these, your "consensus capture is expensive" claim is unverifiable.

#### **3. The "Directory Agreement" Problem Is Still Fuzzy**

You define:

> "D ⊆ ID × PK with predicate Bind(id, pk) = 1 ⟺ (id, pk) ∈ D."

**Problem:** This assumes all honest verifiers agree on the *same* D. But in a distributed system with potential forks, partitions, or censorship, different verifiers might see different views of NVS.

**Specific attacks not addressed:**

1. **Eclipse attack:** An attacker isolates a verifier from the honest Emercoin network and feeds them a fake NVS state. Your snapshot attestation helps, but what if both "independent snapshots" are controlled by the attacker?

2. **Jurisdictional censorship:** Government A forces all local Emercoin nodes to censor WORM entries for certain identities. Verifiers in jurisdiction A see D_A, verifiers in jurisdiction B see D_B. Who's right?

3. **Fork choice during active attack:** During the October 2024 reorg attempt, did *all* clients agree on which chain was canonical, or did some clients temporarily accept the attacker's chain?

**Fix:** You need to specify:

- Fork-choice rule precisely (longest chain with most AuxPoW walls? Most cumulative PoS weight?)
- Synchrony assumptions (e.g., "honest verifiers must connect to at least N geographically diverse Emercoin nodes")
- Explicit partition-resilience claims (or honest admission that partitioned verifiers might diverge)

#### **4. The Skycoin Integration (Section 7) Weakens Your Minimality Claim**

You write:

> "In the Ness / Privateness ecosystem we therefore keep two cryptographic anchors for one entity:
>
> 1. Ed25519 keypair (sk, pk) recorded in WORM / Emer NVS
> 2. One or more Skycoin-type addresses derived from secp256k1"

**This undermines your "bedrock" metaphor.** If your system *also* relies on secp256k1/ECDSA for Skycoin addresses, then you're not just using OWF-minimal Ed25519—you're using *two* signature schemes with *different* security assumptions.

**Questions:**

- If Ed25519 is compromised but secp256k1 is not (or vice versa), what happens?
- Does the "canonical self-spend transaction" actually strengthen security, or is it just proof-of-concept complexity?
- Why not just use *one* signature scheme for everything?

**Fix:** Either:

- Remove Skycoin integration from the "bedrock" layer and clearly mark it as an *optional* higher-layer binding, OR
- Justify why two schemes are necessary (e.g., "Skycoin blockchain uses secp256k1 natively, so interoperability requires this dual-anchor design")

But don't pretend this is "minimal" if you're actually using two independent cryptographic systems.

**Fix:** Publish benchmarks with:

- Realistic network latency (not localhost)
- Load testing results (100, 1k, 10k, 100k concurrent auths)
