SELF-SOVEREING DIGITAL IDENTITIES

PAX: Your Key, Your Identity

An explanation for non‑technical people: how to keep control of who you are online, without giving that power to big platforms or governments.

Today

The Internet Makes You "Rent" Your Identity

Most of today’s logins live on someone else’s computer. If that company bans you, loses your data, or shuts down, your account disappears with it.

  • Websites decide who you are. They can reset passwords or close accounts behind your back.
  • Phone and email are fragile. Lose a number, get your inbox locked, and suddenly you “don’t exist” there.
  • Everyone wants to be your ID card. Banks, governments, and giant tech platforms all want to sit in the middle.
What PAX Changes

You Hold the Only Key

PAX gives you a tiny digital key that only you control. Instead of trusting companies to remember who you are, websites simply check whether you can open the lock with that key.

  • Your key lives with you. On your phone or computer, not on a company’s account server.
  • A public notebook remembers your lock. Once written, nobody can quietly erase or rewrite it.
  • Sites stop being the “owner” of your identity. They just verify that this is really you, right now.
In Plain Words

How PAX Works, Without the Math

1. You create your own key

Your device makes a secret key (like a master house key) and a matching public lock. Only your key opens that lock.

2. The lock is written into a shared notebook e.g blockchain

This notebook is kept by many independent community computers. No single company controls it, they just keep copies of the same pages.

3. To sign in, you open the lock

Instead of typing a password, your device proves: “I hold the right key for this lock.” The website checks that the lock is in the notebook, and that it opens correctly.

No Central Company

No One Can Take the Notebook Away

The important parts of PAX live directly in those shared notebooks and in your key, not in our servers.

  • No single "PAX company" to trust. If one helper service disappears, the records and your key still work.
  • Communities can run their own small helpers. They are replaceable tools, not authorities.
  • Your name isn’t rented. It’s anchored in public records that nobody can secretly edit for you.

In short: PAX separates who you are from which website or chain you use today. Big platforms can still choose whom they serve, but they cannot quietly erase your key or rewrite your past.

For families, communities & small tech projects

Under the hood, PAX anchors your public key in on‑chain records and uses simple transaction patterns as proofs of control. Tiny “bridge” programs read those public records and talk to regular websites using standard login formats. The bridges are replaceable. The non‑replaceable part is your key plus the public notebook that proves it is yours.
