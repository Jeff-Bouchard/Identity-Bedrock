1 Introduction

1.1 The Problem: Institutional Capture

Every deployed digital identity system places institutional keys beneath the
user: certificate authorities, identity providers, key registries, platform opera-
tors. These entities can revoke, deny, surveil, or control identity without the
user's consent.

    The threat model motivating this work assumes an adversary with substan-
tial conventional resources, potential political backing, and full control of tradi-
tional identity infrastructure. The central question is: can identity exist without
institutional keys beneath the individual?

1.2 The Theoretical Answer: Rompel 1990

Rompel proved that OWFs are both necessary and sufficient for SIGEUF-CMA
secure signature schemes [1]. This is the provable minimum--nothing strictly
weaker supports public authentication under standard assumptions.

    Our thesis: fixing OWF as the cryptographic floor and adding only a chain-
agnostic append-only directory produces an identity construction that mini-
mizes institutional capture to zero beneath the individual key.

1.3 The Binding Insight

This work follows a pattern established by prior binding innovations in cryp-
tographic systems: the value lies not in the invention of new primitives but in
recognizing that existing primitives, bound in a specific way, produce emergent
properties none of them exhibits alone. Each component of Identity Bedrock--
OWF-based signatures, append-only directories, bone conduction biometrics--
exists independently in prior literature. The contribution is the binding and the
formal argument for the security properties that binding produces.

1.4 Contributions

Contribution 1 -- Theory. Formalization of a Rompel-grounded, chain-agnostic
identity system. We prove that removing the OWF floor collapses authentica-
tion, and that weakening the directory trades one consensus for another with-
out improving the institutional capture property.

2 Background and Related Work

2.1 Rompel's Theorem (1990)

From a complexity-theoretic perspective, OWFs, universal one-way hash func-
tions (UOWHFs), and SIGEUF-CMA schemes are equivalent in strength [10].
Rompel [1] and later Haitner et al. [2] showed how to construct UOWHFs
from arbitrary OWFs using inaccessible entropy. A modern exposition is given
in [11]. The equivalence is:

                                      OWF  SIGEUF-CMA

under standard definitions [1,16]. To our knowledge, prior work does not ex-
plicitly operationalize this equivalence as the design floor for a deployed iden-
tity system.

2.2 Inaccessible Entropy

Haitner et al. [2] introduce inaccessible entropy to simplify and improve Rompel's
construction. Informally, a function F has inaccessible entropy if the real en-
tropy of F -1--the entropy remaining in the input after observing the output--
significantly exceeds the accessible entropy: the maximum entropy any polynomial-
time adversary can generate among valid preimages.

    We observe in Section 6 that bone conduction independently satisfies an
analogous property--but enforced by physical rather than computational means,
yielding a strictly stronger guarantee.

2.3 Blockchain-Based Identity Systems

Namecoin / EmerDNS. Name-value storage on blockchain. Gap: identity keys
tied to blockchain wallet keys (not chain-agnostic); miners act as an implicit
certificate authority.

W3C DIDs / Ethereum. DID methods, smart contracts, ENS. Gap: DAO gover-
nance keys, registry administrators, and provider dependencies introduce in-
stitutional keys above or beneath the user.

Sovrin / Hyperledger Indy. Permissioned validators, credential schemas. Gap:
steward keys control the network.

Identity Bedrock distinction. Pure OWF floor. Chain-agnostic separation of iden-
tity cryptography from directory consensus. No institutional key beneath the
user by construction.

2.4 Bitcoin AuxPoW and Merged Mining

Auxiliary Proof-of-Work allows Emercoin to be merge-mined with Bitcoin, in-
heriting a portion of Bitcoin's security for non-monetary applications. Kho-
vayko [6] introduces the hack51 coefficient--defined as attack _cost/market_cap--
as a numerically measurable metric for 51% attack resistance. Under this met-
ric, Emercoin's triple consensus (PoS + PoW + merged mining) produces a
coefficient significantly exceeding 1.0: attacking Emercoin requires resources
approaching five times its market capitalization, exceeding the cost of attack-
ing Bitcoin alone at equivalent market cap. We apply the hack51 framework in
Section 7.1.

2.5 Bone Conduction Biometric Authentication

Recent work demonstrates that bone conduction audio carries unique per-individual
characteristics. HeadSonic [13] proposes a bone conduction earphone authenti-
cation system achieving 96.59% balanced accuracy across 60 subjects and demon-
strating resilience against replay and synthesis attacks. HCR-Auth [14] demon-
strates reliable authentication via head contact response. These works establish
empirically that bone conduction authentication resists replay attacks. Neither
provides a formal argument for why. We provide that argument in Section 6.3.

2.6 Skycoin Fiber Consensus

Skycoin Fiber [8] uses announced block publisher keys rather than economic
consensus. Blocks must be signed by announced keys; any number of hostile
nodes up to N - 1 cannot forge blocks without those keys--only the node hold-
ing the publisher key can produce valid blocks. The append-only BoltDB back-
end makes history rewriting impossible and any key compromise maximally
visible.

2.7 Summary of Differences

Identity Bedrock differs from all prior work above in three respects simulta-
neously. First, it is the only system explicitly grounded in Rompel's OWF­
signature equivalence as a design floor. Second, the chain-agnostic architec-
ture decouples identity cryptography from directory consensus: the same key
works across directories, and migration is authenticated by the key itself. Third,
by identifying bone conduction as satisfying the inaccessible entropy property,
we provide the first formal argument for why bone conduction biometrics re-
sist replay--prior work demonstrates this empirically without naming the un-
derlying principle. The PBAT specification is the first token format carrying
physically-bound biometric session evidence alongside a cryptographic iden-
tity proof in a single signed structure.

3.1 Adversary Capabilities

Assumed: Substantial conventional resources and political backing; full con-
trol of traditional identity infrastructure (CAs, IdPs, KYC registries); ability to
outlaw software, seize property, and coerce individuals; classical (polynomial-
time) computational adversary.

    Explicit scope. This work addresses classical adversaries. Post-quantum mi-

3.2 Design Requirements

R1 -- Mathematical irreducibility. OWF as cryptographic floor. No strictly
weaker primitive beneath.

    R2 -- No institutional keys. Individual key is sole cryptographic root of
trust. No CA, IdP, or registry key beneath the user.

    R3 -- Chain-agnostic. Identity cryptography independent of any specific
directory consensus. Portable across append-only substrates.

    R4 -- Quantifiable security. Attack costs explicit and measurable.
    R5 -- Operational verification. Deployed system, not theoretical proposal.
Public verification of all claims.
    R6 -- Physical presence. For deployments requiring physical presence proof,

4 Identity Bedrock Architecture

4.1 The Two-Layer Design

Layer 1: Identity Cryptography (Chain-Agnostic).

                              Seed ( 256 bits) -K-e-yG-en (sk, pk)

              Identity = (id , pk), Authentication = Signsk(challenge)
Properties: OWF-equivalent security (Rompel equivalence); independent of any
blockchain; portable across directory substrates. Default instantiation: Ed25519 [3].
Migration target: ML-DSA-65 [12].

    Layer 2: Directory Substrate (Pluggable).

Definition 2 (Append-Only Directory with Finality). A directory D  ID × PK
is append-only with finality if: (i) once Bind(id , pk) = 1 is confirmed, it cannot be
set to 0 without an attack of quantifiable cost; and (ii) the binding is globally verifiable
by any party without trusting an intermediary.

    Separation invariant. Identity uses its chosen signature scheme; the direc-
tory uses whatever consensus fits the deployment's threat model. The same
identity key can move between directories. No coupling exists between the two
layers.

Setup.

 2. User publishes (id , pk) to directory D.
 3. Wait for finality (directory-dependent).
 4. Binding is global: Bind(id , pk) = 1.

    Challenge-Response.

     (a) Fetch pk s.t. Bind(id , pk) = 1 from D.

     (c) Accept iff both hold.

    No state beyond (id , pk) is held in the directory. No smart contracts. Verifi-
cation is pure mathematics.

4.3 Security Proof

Theorem 1 (Unforgeable Authentication). If the underlying signature scheme is
SIGEUF-CMA secure and D is append-only with finality (Definition 2), then Identity
Bedrock provides unforgeable authentication against any classical adversary.

Proof. Suppose adversary A wins the authentication game: it produces valid
(id , c, ) without holding sk.

lic key pk. It simulates the directory by answering binding queries for id  with

lenge c to A. Since c was chosen fresh and never signed by the legitimate
holder of sk, A's forgery  constitutes an existential forgery. By security of the
scheme:

4.4 Chain-Agnostic Property and Migration

The same (sk, pk) can be published to Emercoin NVS, or any append-only
ledger satisfying Definition 2. Migration is authenticated: the user signs a mi-
gration statement with sk, publishes to D, and verifiers accept after finality.
The old directory retains the historical record.

5.4 Key Generation and Entropy Provisioning

Identity keys are derived from a seed produced by Gibson's UHEPRNG [4]. En-
tropy sources mixed in include: multiple 1024-byte blocks from os.urandom
(1536 bits total); RC4OK output from Emercoin core RNG; CPU timing jitter;
and hardware identifiers (eMMC CID, MAC address, machine-id). The Linux
kernel caps its DRBG state to approximately 256 bits; our multi-source mixing
increases robustness against partial bias without claiming to exceed this ceil-
ing. Security rests on the standard 128-bit classical security floor of the chosen
scheme.

6 Physical Presence Extension

6.1 Motivation

The two-layer Identity Bedrock system addresses forgery (OWF layer) and di-
rectory rewrite (AuxPoW layer). It does not address physical presence: a de-
vice with a stolen key can impersonate the legitimate owner. For deployments
where physical presence is a security requirement, an additional uncondition-
ally guaranteed factor is needed.

6.2 The Channel Symmetry Problem

Every voice authentication system built on air-conducted audio shares a fun-
damental vulnerability: the authentication and replay channels are physically
identical. A microphone captures air pressure waves. A speaker produces air
pressure waves. This is not a software deficiency--it is a property of air-conducted
sound that no liveness detection algorithm can fully overcome.

6.3 Bone Conduction Breaks Channel Symmetry

Bone conduction transmits mechanical vibration through skull bone and soft
tissue to the transducer. Replay requires producing the same mechanical vibra-
tion pattern at the transducer--requiring the same skull geometry, bone density,
tissue distribution, and coupling characteristics as the enrolled individual.

    The physical barrier to replay is the mechanical impedance mismatch at the
air-bone interface. The specific acoustic impedance of air is Zair  415 Pa·s/m;
cortical bone is Zbone  1.5 × 106 Pa·s/m [15]. The pressure transmission coef-
ficient is:

    T  =    4ZairZbone      =  4 × 415 × 1.5 × 106    1.1 × 10-3  (1)
          (Zair + Zbone)2      (415 + 1.5 × 106)2
        Identity Bedrock: Rompel's Minimal Substrate for Sovereign Identity  11

    Approximately 0.11% of acoustic energy from an air-conducted source trans-
mits into bone. The spatial coupling characteristics--which depend on the ge-
ometry of the skull in contact with the transducer--are entirely absent in any
replay attempt.

Remark 1. (1) is a consequence of acoustic physics, not a hardness assumption.
It holds unconditionally, independent of any computational model.

6.4 Bone Conduction as Physical Inaccessible Entropy

Remark 2 (Physical instantiation of inaccessible entropy). The bone conduction chan-
nel produces a distribution--the mechanical resonance signature of the en-
rolled skull--whose entropy is inaccessible to any adversary not in physical
control of the enrolled body in contact with the enrolled transducer. The inac-
cessibility is enforced by (1), not by any computational hardness assumption.
Where Definition 1 requires inaccessibility against PPT adversaries (a computa-
tional bound), bone conduction provides inaccessibility against all adversaries
lacking physical control (an unconditional bound)--a strictly stronger guaran-
tee under any computational model.

Remark 3 (Scope of Remark 2). Remark 2 is an observation connecting a physi-
cal phenomenon to the inaccessible entropy framework of Haitner et al. [2]. A
formal reduction demonstrating that bone conduction channel entropy satisfies
Definition 1 under the standard model remains for future work.

Conclusion

We have presented Identity Bedrock, a deployed identity system grounded in
Rompel's OWF­signature equivalence--arrived at not from reading the theo-
rem first, but from following the requirement of zero institutional capture to its
logical floor. The system achieves no institutional keys beneath the individual
through a chain-agnostic architecture separating identity cryptography from
directory consensus.

    We have further identified bone conduction audio as a physical phenomenon
satisfying the inaccessible entropy property of Haitner et al. [2] (Remark 2), pro-
viding the physical argument for replay resistance that prior biometric work
demonstrates empirically. Integrated as a fourth authentication factor, this pro-
duces the four-factor system of Table 1.

    The canonical on-chain identity record at [19] constitutes operational proof:
the genesis address, blockchain public key, and genesis signature are anchored

in Emercoin NVS via AuxPoW, transitively anchored to Bitcoin, and indepen-
dently auditable by any party without trusting an intermediary. This is not a
paper describing what could be built. It is a paper describing what has been
running since 2020.

A Formal Definitions

Definition 4 (One-Way Function). A function f : {0, 1}  {0, 1} is a one-way
function if it is computable in polynomial time and for all PPT adversaries A:

                             Pr A(f (x))  f -1(f (x))  negl()

where x -$ {0, 1}.
Identity Bedrock: Rompel's Minimal Substrate for Sovereign Identity  19

Definition 5 (SIGEUF-CMA Security). A signature scheme (Gen, Sign, Verify) is
existentially unforgeable under adaptive chosen-message attack if for all PPT
adversaries A with access to a signing oracle:

                     Pr[Verify(pk, m, ) = 1  m / Q]  negl()

where (pk, sk)  Gen(1) and Q is the set of queried messages.

Definition 6 (Physically Inaccessible Biometric Channel). A biometric capture
channel is physically inaccessible if the entropy produced by the channel is inaccessi-
ble to any adversary without physical control of the enrolled subject in contact with the
enrolled transducer, by physical properties of the channel rather than by computational
hardness assumptions.
